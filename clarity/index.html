Top priority: the Hanalei Bay Hale page
9 sessions to /property/hanalei-bay-hale/ in 3 days. 33% of those visitors had a dead click. 44% quick-backed (visited and immediately bounced). 100% triggered a script error. This is one of Pure Kauai's flagship properties and is showing up in Google Ads bids on its own keyword (see referrers table). Every paid click landing here is being burned. Action: open Clarity, filter sessions to this URL, watch 5 recordings to find the broken interaction, then ship the fix this week.
Growth actions ranked by impact
  • 1
    Fix the script error firing on 37% of sessions

    Most likely candidates: a third-party widget (booking, chat, or analytics) failing on iOS Safari, or a JS error in the property template (Hanalei Bay Hale and Kalihiwai Bay Estate both show 100% script error rate, suggesting a template issue). In Clarity, filter by JS Error and watch a recording. Patch the error, then re-pull this report in 7 days to confirm the rate drops below 15%.

    Evidence: 780 errors across 1,464 sessions, 37.23% session penetration
  • 2
    Investigate the 22.5% dead click rate site-wide

    Dead clicks happen when a visitor taps something that looks interactive but isn't. Most often: an image without a link, a heading styled like a button, a "(more)" indicator with no handler. Use Clarity's Dead Click heatmap, prioritize the property pages, and either wire up the missing handler or restyle to look static. This usually returns 1-3% on conversion rate in luxury/travel verticals.

    Evidence: 960 dead-click events across 446 page views
  • 3
    Map paid-ad landing pages to ad copy

    The "best beaches" blog post is receiving paid clicks from a "kauai vacation accommodations" keyword with active time of 4 seconds and scroll depth of 7%. The ad copy and landing page disagree about what the visitor is going to find. Move that keyword to a property index or a destination page that matches the buyer's intent, or write a property-focused landing variant of the blog post.

    Evidence: Multiple paid-traffic URLs showing <10% scroll + <20s active time
  • 4
    Replicate the Hanalei Oasis pattern across mid-tier properties

    Hanalei Oasis pulls 271 sessions in 3 days with no special promotion, ranking 3rd in site-wide traffic. It also serves as an internal referrer to other properties (25 onward navigations). Pull Search Console for this URL, find the top 5 query patterns it ranks on, and apply the same on-page changes (title, H1, schema, internal linking) to 3 mid-tier properties as an A/B test. Conservative read: 1.5x the traffic on 3 properties = ~150 incremental sessions per week.

    Evidence: Hanalei Oasis = 3rd most-visited page, 25 internal-referrer sessions, no paid spend
  • 5
    Set up Clarity Smart Events on the booking funnel

    Right now Clarity sees what page visitors are on but not what they intend. Configure Smart Events for: inquiry-form open, inquiry-form submit, BookingBridge widget open, checkin date selected, calendar interaction. Once those fire, every recording becomes filterable by intent. The reservations team can then watch the 10 highest-intent sessions per day instead of all 480.

THE TOP 1% PLAYBOOK

How the best operators turn Clarity into compounding growth

Clarity is free, so most teams treat it like a free tool. The top 1% use it as the connective tissue between analytics, customer support, paid media, and product. Here is the playbook we follow for Pure Kauai.

01 Watch one recording every morning

Top operators do not run reports. They watch. One recording a day filtered to "purchase intent" sessions (checkin parameter URLs, time on page > 60s). After 30 days, the patterns are obvious. Conversion blockers become visible weeks before they show up in analytics.

02 Tag the booking funnel with Smart Events

Smart Events convert Clarity from "what they did" to "why they came." Tag: inquiry-form open, inquiry-form submit, calendar interaction, price-section scroll, gallery open. Then every recording is filterable by intent.

03 Pair Clarity recordings with GA4 funnel drop-offs

GA4 tells you "30% drop between step 2 and step 3." Clarity tells you why. Cross-reference the GA4 funnel report against Clarity recordings on the same URL pattern + same date range. The dropoff explanation is usually a single dead click or a confusing micro-copy.

04 Create a "high-intent loss" daily segment

Build a Clarity segment that captures: contained a checkin parameter + did not reach /confirmation. These are visitors who got close and didn't convert. Watch 1-2 daily, document the loss reason, ticket it. Over a quarter, this becomes a backlog of high-leverage fixes ordered by frequency.

05 Use Clarity to validate ad creative

When a Google Ads campaign underperforms, do not just look at CTR. Filter Clarity to that campaign's UTMs, watch 3 sessions, see what those visitors actually did. Most "bad campaigns" are good campaigns landing on the wrong page.

06 Mine rage clicks for product roadmap

Rage clicks are the cheapest customer research available. Every cluster of rage clicks is the audience telling you exactly where to invest engineering. Sort by element, address the top 3, repeat monthly.

07 Compare segments instead of averages

Average engagement time hides the story. Instead segment: mobile vs desktop, paid vs organic, new vs returning, US vs international. The deltas show you the highest-leverage problem to solve next.

08 Make Clarity recordings part of customer feedback loops

When a reservations agent gets a confused phone call ("I tried to book but it wouldn't let me"), they should be able to pull that visitor's recording in seconds. Tie Clarity sessions to phone numbers via CallRail's visitor ID and recordings become support superpowers.
