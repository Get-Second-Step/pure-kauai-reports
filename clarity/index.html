Section 1

What Microsoft Clarity is, and how to read this report

Microsoft Clarity is a free behavior analytics tool from Microsoft. Unlike Google Analytics, which tells you what happened (page views, conversions, sessions), Clarity tells you how it happened. Every session is silently recorded as a watchable replay. Every page is overlaid with heatmaps of where visitors clicked, scrolled, and froze. Every frustration moment is detected and counted. This report distils three days of those signals into a single readable picture. Each major number below has an inline definition so you do not need to know Clarity to follow along.

What it measures
Real visitor behavior, not page views
Scrolls, clicks, taps, rage moments, dead taps, time. Everything a visitor physically did.
Why it matters
Finds revenue leaks GA cannot see
A visitor who tapped a broken booking button shows up as "engaged" in GA4. In Clarity they show up as frustrated.
Why us
A luxury rental cannot afford broken UX
Average booking value is > $26,000. Losing even one near-conversion to a dead click is real money.
Section 2 · The health check

How frustrated visitors are, and what it is costing

We computed a Frustration Health Score by combining six Clarity signals (script errors, dead clicks, rage clicks, quick-backs, excessive scrolling, error clicks) into a single 0–100 number where 100 is perfect and below 60 means visible UX problems. Pure Kauai currently scores 55/100. That is not a crisis. But three specific signals are above what a luxury site should accept, and they each have a fix.

55/100
Needs attention

The site works, but it is quietly leaking high-intent visitors.

Three signals are above what we expect for a luxury rental site: 37% of sessions hit a script error (a JavaScript error somewhere on the page, target below 10%), 22.5% experience a dead click (visitor taps something that looks tappable but nothing happens, target below 10%), and 15% quick-back (visitor lands, takes one look, immediately hits back). Together these cost an estimated 8–15% of bookable intent the site is currently swallowing in silence. With average booking value of $26,000 and roughly 60 inbound calls plus form submits per month, even a single recovered booking pays back the fix many times over.

Section 3 · The audience

Pure Kauai's visitor base in the last three days was overwhelmingly mobile, overwhelmingly Apple, and overwhelmingly American. This shapes everything: which browser the site needs to look perfect on, which device size the booking funnel needs to feel native on, and where international demand might be hiding for AEO content to unlock.

Operating system · 1,466 sessions

The Hanalei Oasis anomaly
Hanalei Oasis pulled 271 sessions in 3 days, third-most-visited page in the entire site. No flagship-estate label. No homepage callout. No Google Ads campaign of its own. Best hypothesis: it ranks well on Google for a specific intent we are not yet exploiting (likely "hanalei oasis kauai" or a derivative). The internal-referrer data shows 25 sessions land on Hanalei Oasis and then browse onward to other properties: it is functioning as a top-of-funnel hub even though it was never designed to be one. Immediate action: pull Search Console for this URL, identify the top 5 queries driving it, and replicate the on-page pattern (title, H1, schema, internal linking) across three mid-tier properties as an A/B test. Conservative read: 1.5x the traffic on 3 properties = ~150 incremental sessions per week per property.

Section 8 · What to do next

Seven growth actions, ranked by impact

Each action below is tied to a specific finding in the data. Each includes the What, the Why, the concrete How, and the How we'll measure it worked. The first three are the highest leverage. Five and six are the kind of moves the top 1% of operators make: tooling improvements that compound over time.

  • 1
    Critical · This week

    Fix the script error firing on 37% of sessions

    A JavaScript error on one in three sessions degrades performance, blocks third-party widgets, and is almost certainly the root cause of the dead-click pattern. Most likely culprits: a booking widget, a chat plugin, or an analytics script failing on iOS Safari. Hanalei Bay Hale and Kalihiwai Bay Estate both show 100% script error rate, which suggests a property template issue rather than a site-wide one.

    How In Clarity, go to Recordings, filter by "JavaScript Error". Watch three recordings. The error name will appear in the network panel. Patch it, then re-pull this report in 7 days to confirm the rate drops below 15%.
    Evidence: 780 errors across 1,464 sessions = 37.23% session penetration
  • 2
    Critical · Next two weeks

    Investigate the 22.5% site-wide dead click rate

    Dead clicks happen when a visitor taps something that looks interactive but is not. The most common offenders on rental sites: an image without a link wrapping it, a heading styled like a button, a "(read more)" indicator that does nothing, a chevron icon that suggests expansion. Industry benchmark for a luxury vacation rental is below 10%.

    How Open Clarity's Dead Click heatmap. Sort pages by dead-click count, focus on the property pages first. For each pattern, either wire up the missing link or restyle the element to look static. Returns typically 1–3% on conversion rate in this vertical.
    Evidence: 960 dead-click events across 446 page views
  • 3
    High · This month

    Re-map paid landing pages to ad intent

    The "best beaches" blog post is receiving paid clicks from a "kauai vacation accommodations" keyword, then showing 4 seconds active time and 7% scroll depth. The ad copy and landing page are not telling the same story. This is a fixable Google Ads waste.

    How Either move the keyword to a properties index or a destination landing page that matches the buyer's intent, or write a property-focused landing variant of the blog post. Test both with a 50/50 split. A/B for two weeks before deciding.
    Evidence: Multiple paid-traffic URLs showing <10% scroll + <20s active time
  • 4
    Strategic · This quarter

    Replicate the Hanalei Oasis pattern across mid-tier properties

    Hanalei Oasis pulled 271 sessions in 3 days with no promotion, ranking third in site-wide traffic. It also functions as an internal referrer to other properties (25 onward navigations). Whatever Google has decided about Hanalei Oasis, the pattern is replicable.

    How Pull Search Console for this URL. Identify the top 5 query patterns it ranks on. Apply the same on-page pattern (title structure, H1, schema, internal linking, image alt text) to three mid-tier properties as an A/B test. Measure organic traffic to those properties at 30 and 60 days.
    Evidence: Hanalei Oasis = 3rd most-visited page, 25 internal-referrer sessions, no paid spend
  • 5
    Tooling · Setup once, compounds forever

    Configure Clarity Smart Events on the booking funnel

    Right now Clarity sees which page each visitor reached but not what they intended to do. Smart Events are conditional triggers: when a specific selector is clicked, or a specific page is reached, Clarity tags the session. With Smart Events configured, every session becomes filterable by intent.

    How Configure events for: inquiry-form open, inquiry-form submit, BookingBridge widget open, calendar date selected, gallery image clicked. Setup takes about 30 minutes. After two weeks of data accumulation, the reservations team can watch the 10 highest-intent sessions per day instead of trying to skim 480.
    No cost, 30 minutes to configure, compounds every day after
  • 6
    Tooling · The single most valuable Clarity practice

    Build a saved "checkin parameter + low scroll" segment

    A visitor who reaches /property/X/?checkin=… has selected specific dates. They are actively shopping. If their scroll depth is below 30% on that page, they are either price-shocked, availability-shocked, or unsure how to proceed. Every recording in this segment is a near-conversion loss in slow motion.

    How Create a Clarity segment for "URL contains checkin AND scroll < 30%". Review one recording per day. Document the loss reason. Three weeks of this practice produces a prioritized backlog of conversion-rate fixes that pays for itself many times over.
    Evidence: Several checkin-parameter URLs already show 35–48% scroll, edge cases warrant deeper review
  • 7
    Conversion · This quarter

    Surface the About page inside property pages

    58% scroll depth and 67s active time on /about/ is best-in-class for a brand page. People who read About convert better in luxury verticals (research, trust, decide). Right now visitors have to navigate to find it.

    How Add a "Why Pure Kauai" callout in the property-page sidebar (one paragraph, founder photo, one trust line). Visitors who are evaluating a property build trust without leaving the funnel.
    Evidence: /about/ = 18 sessions, 58.4% scroll, 67s active time
THE TOP 1% PLAYBOOK

How the best operators turn Clarity into compounding growth

Clarity is free, so most teams treat it like a free tool. The top 1% use it as connective tissue between analytics, customer support, paid media, and product. Each play below is tied to one of the findings in this report. Most cost nothing. All of them compound.

01 Watch one recording every morning

Reports tell you what. Recordings tell you why. One recording a day filtered to "purchase intent" sessions (checkin parameter URLs, time on page over 60s) creates pattern recognition that no dashboard can produce. After 30 days, conversion blockers become obvious weeks before analytics surfaces them.

Applies to Pure Kauai · especially the /property/…?checkin= traffic
02 Tag the booking funnel with Smart Events

Smart Events convert Clarity from "what they did" to "why they came." Tag every meaningful action: form open, form submit, calendar interaction, gallery open. Then every recording is filterable by intent and stage. This is the single highest-leverage tooling improvement available.

Action 5 above. Most undervalued Clarity feature.
03 Pair Clarity recordings with GA4 funnel drop-offs

GA4 tells you "30% drop between step 2 and step 3." Clarity tells you why. Cross-reference the GA4 funnel report against Clarity recordings on the same URL pattern and date range. The dropoff explanation is usually a single dead click or a confusing micro-copy.

Especially useful once GA4 access for the service account is granted.
04 Create a daily "high-intent loss" segment

A Clarity segment that captures: contained a checkin parameter, time on page over 60s, did not reach /confirmation. These are visitors who got close and did not convert. Watching one or two per day, documenting the loss reason, ticketing each, produces a backlog of high-leverage fixes within a quarter.

Action 6 above. The most useful single segment for Pure Kauai.
05 Use Clarity to validate ad creative

When a Google Ads campaign underperforms, do not just check the CTR. Filter Clarity to that campaign's UTM, watch three sessions, see what those visitors actually did after the click. Most "bad campaigns" are good campaigns landing on the wrong page.

Action 3 above. The "best beaches" landing is exactly this pattern today.
06 Mine rage clicks for product roadmap

Rage clicks are the cheapest customer research available. Every cluster of rage clicks is the audience telling you exactly where to invest engineering. Sort the rage-click heatmap by element, address the top 3 sources, repeat monthly. Each cycle removes a friction the team did not know existed.

Pure Kauai has 49 rage clicks in 3 days. Small but worth a sweep.
07 Compare segments instead of averages

Average engagement time hides the story. Instead segment: mobile vs desktop, paid vs organic, new vs returning, US vs international. The deltas show the highest-leverage problem to solve next. The Pure Kauai data already suggests iOS Safari is the segment that needs the most attention.

Next pull will include per-source frustration breakdown.
08 Tie Clarity to phone calls via CallRail

When a reservations agent gets a confused phone call ("I tried to book and it would not let me"), they should be able to pull that visitor's recording in seconds. Tying Clarity sessions to phone numbers via CallRail's visitor ID turns Clarity into a support superpower.

Pairs with the CallRail data already flowing into BookingBridge.

Glossary · the Clarity terms used in this report

Skip past the chart titles and you have already met all of these. Here they are in one place for reference.

Session
One continuous visit by one person. A visitor who comes back tomorrow counts as a second session.
Distinct user
One unique visitor, counted once across all of their sessions.
Pages per session
How many pages the average visitor viewed before leaving.
Scroll depth
How far down a page the visitor got. 100% means they reached the footer.
Active time
How long the visitor was actually interacting (scrolling, clicking, moving), not just leaving the tab open.
Dead click
A tap or click on something that looks interactive but does nothing. The single most actionable signal of frustration.
Rage click
Three or more rapid clicks on the same element. The visitor expected a response and is now angry.
Quick-back
The visitor lands on a page, looks at it, and immediately hits the back button. Almost always an ad/landing mismatch or a slow page.
Script error
A JavaScript error fired somewhere on the page. Often invisible to the visitor, but it breaks widgets, forms, and tracking.
Excessive scrolling
The visitor scrolled up and down rapidly trying to find something. A sign of unclear navigation or missing content.
Smart Events
Custom Clarity triggers that tag sessions when a specific element is clicked or a specific URL is reached. The setup that turns Clarity into a CRO tool.
Heatmap
A visualization of where on a page visitors clicked, scrolled, or moved their cursor. Three types: click, scroll, area.
