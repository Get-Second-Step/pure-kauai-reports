What "Brand 51%" actually means
These are people typing "pure kauai" directly into Google — they already know the brand by name. The ad guarantees Pure Kauai shows up at the top instead of competitors, OTAs (Vrbo, Airbnb), or affiliate sites buying that term.

So the 51% reflects three things layered together: (1) brand awareness built by organic marketing, past stays, word-of-mouth, social, and PR; (2) the ad capturing that demand before competitors do; and (3) high intent — these searchers know what they want.

Mental model: Brand spend isn't creating demand — it's defending demand the rest of the marketing engine creates. That's why the ROAS looks so high. It's cheap insurance against losing a known buyer to a competitor's ad.

PMax & phone calls — why we can't connect them today
Can we prove which calls came from PMax? Here's what's blocking us and the fix.
Why PMax shows 0 bookings on $3,107 spent
PMax can drive both web bookings and phone calls — but right now Google Ads can't see either of them properly:

Web bookings are missed because the bookings happen in Hostfully, not on the website. The fix is BookingBridge V2 (in active build) — pushes confirmed Hostfully bookings back to Google Ads as offline conversions.

Phone-call bookings are missed because CallRail isn't sending call data back to Google Ads. CallRail tracks the call (caller number, duration, source), but unless the CallRail → Google Ads integration is wired up, Google never sees those calls.

Net effect: PMax looks broken on paper. It might actually be working — we just have no way to prove it. That changes in May once both pipes are live.

Enable CallRail Dynamic Number Insertion (DNI) on purekauai.com
Different tracking number per traffic source — Google Ads, organic, direct, social. Same caller, different numbers. ~30 minutes.

What we'll see after 30 days
PMax reports will show 2 conversion types: "Web Booking" (BookingBridge) and "Phone Call" (CallRail). We'll be able to say with certainty: "PMax drove $X in web bookings and Y phone calls last month." Right now we're guessing.
The single biggest opportunity for Q2 — already in motion
The most important fix in the account is BookingBridge V2: pushing confirmed Hostfully bookings back into Google Ads as offline conversions, so the bidding algorithm finally optimizes for real revenue instead of proxy on-site events.

Status: V1 is already live (capturing inquiries with click ID). V2 (the upload back to Google Ads) is in active build — shipping by May 15. Once it's live, every other Q2 optimization compounds — especially PMax, which today looks broken because it can't see what it's actually doing.

All Q1 bookings — and a clear word on the 19 from Google Ads
Google Ads' dashboard reports 19 bookings worth $342,907 for Q1. Hostfully shows 50 confirmed bookings worth $1,207,131 in the same window — the 19 from Google Ads are a subset of these.

Why we don't show "the 19" alone — and why that's the right call: tagging a specific booking as "this one came from Google Ads" requires the click ID (GCLID) captured at the moment the guest clicked the ad to be matched back to the Hostfully booking. That match infrastructure (BookingBridge) was being built from scratch during Q1 — we shipped first fixes in March, and a second round of improvements in April. Q1 attribution was therefore partial. Picking a specific 19 today would mean filtering by amount profile (an educated guess), not by actual click attribution. We'd rather show the full picture honestly than a 19-row list that looks precise but is really our best estimate.

What you can do right now: Cody can open Google Ads → Tools → Conversions → segment by date, then match the 19 GA conversion timestamps + amounts to specific rows below. That's the cleanest manual path until V2 lands.

What changes next: BookingBridge V2 (shipping May 15) closes the click → booking loop end-to-end. From then on, every confirmed Hostfully booking carries its source attribution baked in — Q2 reports won't need this caveat at all.
